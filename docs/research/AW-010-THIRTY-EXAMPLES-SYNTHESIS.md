## 3. Observed pricing and packaging patterns

### 3.1 Small bounded outputs cluster in low-thousands RUB

Across the 33-example set, many bounded one-off packages sit roughly in the **2,990–6,500 RUB** range:
- table cleanup;
- one bug/small code change;
- document conversion/extraction;
- one-site parsing;
- price-list reconciliation;
- compact research/report output.

This is a public-offer pattern, not a realized transaction-price distribution.

### 3.2 Recurrence and implementation raise ticket size

Offers that install an ongoing system rather than deliver one bounded result commonly move into higher price bands:
- multi-source monitoring and alerting;
- reusable automations;
- managed AI analysis;
- custom parser/AI pipelines.

Observed examples range from approximately **10k–30k+ RUB**, with managed AI scenarios reaching **78k RUB** in this sample.

### 3.3 Buyers are already shown explicit unit boundaries

Common package boundaries repeatedly observed:
- one file / source / site / scenario;
- N rows, records, products, pages or fields;
- one small code change;
- named output schema/file type;
- one revision round;
- ambiguous/conflicting records separated instead of guessed;
- sample-based or before/after checks.

These are useful design inputs for `Job`, `Result`, `Evidence` and `Acceptance`.

## 4. Acceptance patterns

### Stronger machine-verifiable patterns

The most verification-friendly public offers tend to expose:
- exact row/field limits;
- schema/formats;
- deterministic matching rules;
- before/after row counts;
- explicit conflict/error outputs;
- repeatable scripts plus sample result;
- monitoring history/deltas.

This pattern appears frequently in J-02/J-03/J-06-like work.

### Weaker / more subjective patterns

Research, strategy and broad analysis offers usually define a document/report but leave quality judgment more subjective. Human expertise and interpretation remain a material part of acceptance.

## 5. Counterexamples and reasons not to over-generalize

1. **Some buyers clearly buy automation rather than work output.** A 13k–78k RUB automation/managed-analysis package transfers reusable capability to the buyer; a per-Job market is not automatically superior.
2. **Open-ended research is not a clean first autonomous Job.** Output can be formally delivered yet still fail on usefulness, judgment or missing context.
3. **Lead/contact-base work introduces data-boundary risk.** Even when offers state public-source constraints, Arvectum Work must not infer that all contact enrichment is legally or operationally low-risk.
4. **Procurement-related work can cross from data processing into judgment.** Specification normalization can be bounded; legal compliance, bid decisions, analog approval and submissions are materially different.
5. **Code work can require privileged environments.** A small bug fix is commercially atomic but may need source access, secrets, production context or deployment authority, increasing safety/verification cost.
6. **Marketplace listing != demand.** Public offers prove packaging/supply exists; they do not prove purchase frequency, repeat purchase or realized margin.

## 6. Plausible wedges — hypotheses, not selection

### Wedge hypothesis A — bounded document/data transformation

Candidate families: `J-02` + `J-03`.

Why it deserves buyer testing:
- repeated public fixed-price packaging;
- clear row/page/schema boundaries;
- ambiguity can be explicitly flagged;
- deterministic samples/checks are feasible;
- local/self-hosted execution can be operationally meaningful for sensitive business documents.

Unknowns:
- real buyer frequency and budget;
- accuracy threshold buyers require;
- human verification cost;
- whether confidentiality/data constraints permit the desired execution contour;
- positive contribution margin after rework.

### Wedge hypothesis B — recurring monitoring/reconciliation

Candidate families: `J-06` plus parts of `J-03`.

Why it deserves buyer testing:
- recurrence is intrinsic to the task;
- result/evidence can be represented as timestamped deltas/history;
- repeat purchase may be more natural than repeated one-off ordering;
- acceptance can often be tied to coverage, schedule and detected changes.

Unknowns:
- source stability and anti-bot constraints;
- whether buyers prefer owning the automation instead of purchasing results;
- operating/support burden;
- acceptable failure/latency rate.

### Wedge hypothesis C — bounded code fixes

Candidate family: `J-04`.

Commercial atomization is clearly observable, but it is not yet preferred because execution may require repository credentials, environment reproduction and potentially consequential deployment. Buyer interviews and a stricter safety/acceptance design would be required before using it as the first paid Worker experiment.

## 7. Managed service vs marketplace remains unresolved

The evidence does **not** show that a two-sided marketplace is needed. Current public substitutes support at least three plausible operating models:

1. fixed-price human freelance marketplace;
2. managed automation / implementation service;
3. subscription or reusable automation capability.

Arvectum Work's fourth model — `buy a verified machine-executed result` — remains a hypothesis. A managed machine-work service may be the cheaper Discovery vehicle before matching/liquidity is proven.
